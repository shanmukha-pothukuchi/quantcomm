How FloodAlpha Works

Step 1

A radar pass lands

Sentinel-1 sees through storm cloud. The Copernicus publication time of the first post-storm image is our event clock, so nothing downstream peeks ahead.

Step 2

Water gets mapped

Post-minus-pre backscatter change on matching orbits, with permanent water and steep slopes masked out. Output: a flood mask, polygons and tract-level percent flooded.

Step 3

Exposure gets scored

Every bank's share of deposits within 500 m of mapped flooding, using the Summary of Deposits vintage published before the storm.

Read the Results. Honestly.

Twelve storms, one pipeline. Pick a metric to compare them.

Flooded area from the first post-storm passes generated 2026-10-17 21:55 UTC
    Verdict

    The radar works. The alpha doesn't, yet.

    • Flood maps are weakly but consistently informative: AUC 0.54 vs a 0.51 baseline, held-out score 0.607 after tuning.
    • Only ~19 of ~1,400 storm-bank pairs carry non-zero satellite exposure, so the market test has little to work with.
    • The long-short backtest made 2 trades and lost on both (−0.70 % at 5 days, −1.04 % at 20 days, 10 bp/side).

    A clean null is still a result. Every threshold, PD uplift and buffer is justified in the methodology.
